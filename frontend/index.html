The Real Problem

Slow, Subjective Diagnosis

Reading a coronary angiogram to spot blockages requires a trained cardiologist and takes time. In many hospitals, especially outside major cities, immediate access to a specialist isn't always possible. Meanwhile, patients often leave with a report full of jargon they don't understand.

01

Specialist Shortage

Rural and smaller hospitals may not have a cardiologist on-site for immediate review.

02

Subjective Reads

Two doctors can estimate blockage severity differently just by eye.

03

Patient Confusion

Reports are written for doctors, not patients — leaving people anxious and unclear.
