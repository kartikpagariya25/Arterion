CPR & Cardiac Arrest Survival Skills

Knowing these steps can double or triple a person's chance of survival before an ambulance arrives.

This is general awareness information, not a substitute for certified first-aid/CPR training. In a real emergency, call 108 immediately and perform CPR only if you are trained or being guided by emergency dispatch.

Step by Step

What To Do When Someone Collapses

1

Check for Response & Breathing

Tap the person firmly on the shoulders and shout. Look for normal breathing for no more than 10 seconds. Gasping or irregular breathing is NOT normal breathing.

2

Call for Help Immediately

Shout for someone nearby to call 108 (or dial it yourself on speaker) and to bring an AED (automated external defibrillator) if one is available nearby.

Do this before starting CPR
3

Start Chest Compressions

Kneel beside the person. Place the heel of one hand on the center of the chest, other hand on top, fingers interlocked. Push hard and fast — 100–120 compressions per minute, at least 5cm deep, letting the chest fully recoil between pushes.

4

Use an AED If Available

Turn it on and follow its voice prompts — it will guide pad placement and tell you when to stand clear and shock. AEDs are designed to be used safely by untrained bystanders.

5

Continue Until Help Arrives

Keep compressing without stopping until paramedics take over, the person starts breathing normally, or you are physically unable to continue. Switch with another trained person every 2 minutes if possible to avoid fatigue.

Method Reference

Hands-Only CPR vs. Choking Response

Two different emergencies, two different techniques — know which one applies.

Hands-Only CPR (Cardiac Arrest)

  1. Confirm unresponsiveness and abnormal/absent breathing.
  2. Call 108 / get someone else to call.
  3. Push hard and fast in the center of the chest, 100–120/min.
  4. Do not stop to check for breathing repeatedly — keep going.
  5. Rescue breaths are optional for untrained bystanders; continuous compressions are still effective.

Choking Response (Abdominal Thrusts)

  1. Ask "Are you choking?" — if they can't speak/cough/breathe, act.
  2. Stand behind them, wrap arms around their waist.
  3. Make a fist above the navel, grasp it with your other hand.
  4. Give quick, upward abdominal thrusts until the object is expelled.
  5. If they become unresponsive, lower them down and begin CPR immediately.

We strongly encourage getting certified in CPR and first aid through a recognized provider (Red Cross, St. John Ambulance, or a local hospital training program) — hands-on practice builds the muscle memory this page cannot.
